How direction changes floating wind response
============================================

The problem
-----------

A Y-shaped floating platform does not present the same geometry to every incoming wind and wave direction. Motions and component loads can therefore vary with heading. Understanding that variation is important when deciding which conditions to examine in a floating wind design.

The contribution
----------------

This study investigates a 10 MW Y-shaped semi-submersible wind turbine using a 1:70 model in combined wind-tunnel and wave-flume testing. Calibration establishes the wind and wave conditions, while the model turbine is arranged to represent the target thrust. Still-water, wind, wave and combined cases examine the influence of incoming direction. Comparisons with an OpenFAST model connect the measurements to numerical system analysis. The different response measures show why a heading that is favourable for one motion or load need not be favourable for another.

Engineering significance
------------------------

The experiments provide physical evidence for including direction in early floating-system assessment and for checking a numerical model against measured behaviour. Their interpretation retains the test boundaries: the wind and waves are co-directional in the tested cases, so the results do not establish behaviour in crossed wind and wave conditions. Thrust matching at model scale also does not reproduce every full-scale aerodynamic or control effect. The study supports directional response assessment within those conditions, rather than demonstrating full-scale turbine performance.
