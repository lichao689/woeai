Comparing floaters over a service life
======================================

The problem
-----------

Comparing one selected floating-platform design with another can mix the merits of a concept with the quality of its chosen dimensions. Short design cases also provide only a partial view of systems that experience many environmental conditions over a service life. A comparison should examine the alternatives each concept can offer under consistent assumptions.

The contribution
----------------

This study combines reduced-order dynamic modelling, long-term environmental conditions, surrogate modelling and multiobjective optimisation. It applies the framework to Y-shaped and square semi-submersible substructures for floating wind turbines. Rather than reporting only a single preferred configuration, the optimisation develops sets of designs that express trade-offs between construction cost and long-term response or fatigue measures. Comparing these sets helps distinguish differences between the concepts from differences caused by a particular dimension choice.

Engineering significance
------------------------

The approach gives early design comparison a longer time horizon and makes competing objectives visible. It can help an engineer ask which response measure drives a cost increase, or whether a concept remains competitive after its dimensions are optimised. The conclusions depend on the environmental distribution, response model, design variables, constraints and cost assumptions used. They identify trade-offs within that comparison, rather than a universally best platform. Detailed structural design and project-specific verification remain further stages beyond this concept-selection framework.
