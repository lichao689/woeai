Designing a concrete floating platform
======================================

The problem
-----------

A concrete floating wind platform must balance buoyancy, motion, mooring loads and structural strength. Changing its main dimensions can improve one response while increasing cost or worsening another. Assessing dynamic performance alone is therefore insufficient to judge a platform concept.

The contribution
----------------

This study proposes a modular reinforced-concrete semi-submersible concept and connects its main-dimension design to long-term dynamic analysis. Reduced-order modelling and NSGA-II multiobjective optimisation explore cost and performance trade-offs. Selected designs are compared with the OO-Star reference platform under the study’s assumptions. The investigation then considers overall structural strength, including the role of prestressing, so that the dynamic design is also examined as a load-bearing concrete structure. The analysis distinguishes broad stress patterns from local stress concentrations near structural details.

Engineering significance
------------------------

The work places material choice, platform geometry and mooring behaviour within a connected design process. Its contribution is a concept-level route from performance objectives to a structural assessment, with particular attention to interactions that would be missed by optimising the platform alone. The findings do not establish a construction-ready design or a guaranteed project cost. Local joints and stress concentrations need further detailed design, and reported cost comparisons retain the paper’s scope and assumptions. This distinction is essential when moving from an optimisation result towards an engineered floating foundation.
